Every year, WinPlus walks many students through one of the most important seasons of their lives, and every student's story is worth sharing. Today we want to share one student's story. In just six months he went from uncertainty and doubt to confidently accepting an offer from the US university he wanted.

## <span class="h-num">01</span> From uncertain to certain: switching from Europe to the US

The student originally planned to apply to ETH Zurich, believing Europe's rigorous system suited him best. After a study trip to Europe, he began to reconsider. European universities are rigorous and high-quality, but he realized they might not fully match his goals for interdisciplinary learning and a global outlook.

What really pushed him toward the US was attending the UN Climate Change Conference (COP28) that same year. WinPlus co-founder Cathy Guo organized the trip end to end and made sure students had a chance to present on the international stage. In Dubai she also hosted a Harvard alumni gathering, where the student met graduates of top US universities and talked with them in depth about his interests and plans. That immersive academic and cultural exchange planted the idea of studying in the US.

At COP28 he presented his environmental project on coal gangue to government officials and experts from around the world, representing Chinese youth. The experience convinced him that the diverse, open and innovative environment of US universities could better broaden his horizons, and that they offer clear advantages in academic resources, lab facilities and interdisciplinary research.

## <span class="h-num">02</span> A winter study tour and company visits: exploring careers

After COP28, Cathy Guo invited the student to join WinPlus's winter study tour on the US East Coast. More than a year later, it is still vivid for him. A dozen or so days felt like a whole semester. It was his first time in the US: from Boston south through New York and Philadelphia, and on to Pittsburgh and Washington, D.C., visiting more than ten top universities including Harvard, MIT, Yale, Brown and Penn, as well as tech companies, cultural institutions and UN Headquarters.

"As soon as I landed in Boston and saw Ms. Guo and Mr. Pan, and all those schools I'd only heard of, I was blown away," the student recalls. "Especially walking between Harvard and Boston University for those few days, I felt a really strong university culture."

Cathy remembers visiting Carnegie Mellon together: "It made a big impression. CMU is not only top in AI and engineering, its music school is among the best in the country too. Later, back at MIT, I noticed that many engineering students are excellent musicians as well."

Beyond academics, Cathy built in experiences that mix learning and fun: going up New York's tallest building, touring the UN, seeing a Broadway show. She wants students not only to learn, but to widen their horizons and feel how rich culture and life's possibilities can be.

Throughout the tour, she brought in more than thirty mentors and older students from Meta, Google, Silicon Valley venture capital and other backgrounds, who shared honest, thoughtful stories about careers, choosing a major and life. Back in Beijing, the student began to rethink his path. "The trip was short, but it had a huge impact on me," he recalls. "I started to realize I had many more choices, and to believe I could go further." He decided to apply to US universities.

## <span class="h-num">03</span> Planning activities and building a profile in six months

When the student decided to apply to the US, it was already the end of May in 11th grade, and time was extremely tight. We assessed his background in detail. He had done many extracurricular activities, but without a system aimed at US applications. His GPA was 3.5, and his TOEFL and SAT scores weren't ready. We quickly built a comprehensive plan covering testing, research, the arts and community service.

<ol class="steps-list">
<li><div><strong>Raise test scores</strong><span>We matched him with experienced one-on-one TOEFL and SAT teachers. From late May to December he studied intensively and earned strong scores on both, a clear boost to his competitiveness.</span></div></li>
<li><div><strong>Do research and publish</strong><span>He had worked on a Chinese Academy of Sciences project that had paused but could continue. Once environmental science was set as his major, we advised him to reconnect with the original professor (a remote sensing and GIS specialist) and helped him set research goals and plan the outreach.</span></div></li>
<li><div><strong>Build out arts and service</strong><span>He curated a solo photography exhibition and a professional portfolio, submitted to photography magazines, and organized and played in charity concerts at Beijing Children's Hospital.</span></div></li>
<li><div><strong>Write an honest, effective essay</strong><span>Two months of work with lead essay mentor Josh, a former Yale admissions officer, on a personal statement built around film photography.</span></div></li>
</ol>

While choosing his topic and writing the paper, the student stayed in close contact with Cedric Pan. He sent drafts, and our team gave feedback together to sharpen them. He ultimately submitted and published the paper in an academic journal, completing the full research process. "Universities would rather see research a student reached out for and earned, even free resources, than a project that was bought," says Cedric. "A Chinese Academy of Sciences background like this is especially convincing."

WinPlus has deep experience guiding research. For students aiming at MIT, for example, we have helped them analyze professors' work, write outreach letters, request to sit in on classes, and ultimately complete lab research and earn a professor's recommendation. Whether the field is environmental science, AI engineering or ocean engineering, we combine each student's interests and existing resources to build authentic, effective research experience. That is exactly what admissions officers value.

## <span class="h-num">04</span> An essay that is honest, and works

Polishing the main essay was a core task in the student's application. We started at the beginning of summer and matched him with our lead essay mentor, Josh, who has extensive writing-coaching experience and previously worked in Yale's admissions office, so he knows the depth of thought and style admissions officers look for.

We always stress that the personal statement isn't a list of experiences. It is real self-expression. Before choosing a topic, we interviewed and guided the student several times to identify which experiences had shaped him most and which reflections best represented his values. We agreed that his long-standing practice of shooting film showed not only his sensitivity to beauty and record-keeping, but his deep understanding of failure, waiting and uncertainty. That became the central metaphor of his essay.

From concept to final draft took nearly two months. With Josh, the student worked out how to tell a story about "a small thing in daily life" in under 500 words and lift it to a philosophy of life. We coached him to "see the big in the small," mapping details like "you can't control film exposure" and "a failed negative can't be retaken" onto his setbacks and efforts across three years of high school.

Josh and Cedric helped him make the language concise and rhythmic, paying special attention to structure and emotional arc, so an admissions officer would be drawn in by the first paragraph and remember the last. We also checked that his activity list and intended major aligned with the essay's theme, so his story, abilities, interests and potential all pointed the same way.

The essay became the most moving part of his application. It captured his personality and persistence, and showed his resilience in the face of uncertainty and failure: exactly the authenticity, reflection and growth mindset US universities value.

<p>I think the most important purpose of the essay is to be a window into your own thinking. Photography is a very important part of my life; I shoot almost every month, even every week. <p>People often ask me: digital cameras are so advanced now, why do you still shoot film? I ask myself that too. <p>I started projecting that onto life more broadly. In three years of high school, there were many times I worked hard and didn't get the result I wanted. If the process is real and the effort is total, it deserves recognition.</p>
<p>With that core idea, Josh, Mr. Pan and I had many in-depth discussions, not only on how to bring the ideas into the essay, but on which details and moments could work as small things that reveal big ones: which photography experience to use, how to express emotion through rhythm, where to turn and reveal the theme. Every part was carefully refined.</p>
<p>This essay was my deepest look at myself. It tells a story, and it also holds my understanding of failure, persistence and growth. The teachers' guidance and support let me turn those complex thoughts into words with real momentum.</p></dd>

## <span class="h-num">05</span> The results, and what he gained

After careful planning and preparation, the student received offers from 12 US universities, including UC Davis, Rutgers and Ohio State, and won a scholarship of up to **$80,000 from UC Riverside**.

Throughout the process we emphasized that authenticity matters more than outcomes. What the student gained during application season went far beyond the offers: he learned to handle pressure, manage the anxiety of applying, and build a more positive, optimistic mindset.
